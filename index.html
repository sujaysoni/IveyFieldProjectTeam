The Mission

A consulting engagement built for strategic relevance.

The Ivey Field Project is the cornerstone of experiential learning at Ivey because it places a six-person team inside a real client context, where the work must withstand executive scrutiny and lead to actionable recommendations.

Executive summary

The IFP is a cross-enterprise consulting experience in which students partner with an organization to study an issue, problem, or opportunity of operational, organizational, or strategic significance. The work culminates in evidence-based recommendations and an implementation-oriented final report for senior management.

  • Focus on innovation, disruption, large-scale change, and strategic decision-making.
  • Blend internal diagnostics with external market, industry, and competitor insights.
  • Translate analysis into a practical plan for action rather than an academic summary.

What is IFP?

The cornerstone of the Ivey experience.

The IFP is a professional consulting relationship — not a classroom simulation. Each engagement is built around significance, multidisciplinary analysis, and implementation readiness, with the client's senior leadership as the primary audience.

Strategic scope

IFP projects address issues with organization-wide implications — from expansion and acquisition decisions to full strategy reassessments and operational redesigns.

Senior guidance

Each team works with a faculty supervisor and an Executive-in-Residence who pressure-test assumptions, methodologies, and the practicality of the final recommendations.

Client impact

The end product is a professional final report and presentation intended to help the client’s leadership team move forward with clearer evidence and a stronger plan for action.

Methodology

How we approach the work.

Our process mirrors what a client would expect from a professional consulting team: rigorous discovery, evidence-based analysis, and implementation-oriented thinking at every stage.

Primary Research

Interviews, stakeholder conversations, and issue framing help us surface the realities behind the brief and identify what matters most to decision-makers.

Secondary Insights

Industry structure, market dynamics, competitor moves, and operating benchmarks provide the external context needed to test the client’s options.

Actionable Implementation

We move beyond diagnosis by defining practical recommendations, sequencing priorities, and clarifying the path from insight to execution.

Company Benefits

What your organization gains from an IFP engagement.

  • Obtain a fresh, unbiased perspective on a significant business issue, strategic opportunity, operational problem, or organizational challenge.
  • Work with a highly motivated and intelligent team of Ivey MBA students dedicated to your mandate.
  • Receive a professional consulting report supervised by world-class faculty and experienced mentors.
  • Gain exposure to the latest business ideas, tools, and frameworks directly applicable to your situation.
  • Access guidance from an accomplished Executive-in-Residence with relevant industry experience.
  • Evaluate students’ strategic capabilities first-hand — and identify future talent for your organization.
  • Partner with Ivey in the hands-on education of tomorrow’s business leaders.

Engagement Criteria

Client applications are assessed against the following criteria.

  • Strategic significance: The scope represents an issue or opportunity with organization-wide implications — such as new market or product expansion, acquisition evaluation, post-acquisition integration, or a full strategy reassessment.
  • Client commitment: The client is prepared to invest meaningful time with the team, share relevant financial and operational information, and reimburse students for direct project costs (travel, supplies, and related expenses). Students cannot be financially compensated for their work.
  • Timeline fit: The project scope and deliverables align with the structure and timeline of the IFP program.
